How we decide what gets listed.

A product either meets our standard or it doesn't. No scores. No tiers.

Last reviewed: June 2025

Why nontoxic is complicated

Regulation lags the science by decades. An ingredient can be legal, widely used, and still be an endocrine disruptor, a suspected carcinogen, or simply untested at the concentrations found in everyday products. "Nontoxic" isn't a legal category — it's a judgment call, and most brands are the ones making it about themselves.

We don't think that's a fair trade. So we built a standard that starts from the opposite assumption: an ingredient is treated as a problem until a brand can show otherwise, not the other way around. That standard is public, applied the same way to every brand regardless of size or relationship with us, and revisited whenever the science moves.

When in doubt, it's out.

What we always block

These ingredients disqualify a product outright, regardless of concentration, formulation, or brand reputation.

Synthetic fragrance Parabens Sulfates Formaldehyde and releasers Petroleum derivatives PEGs Synthetic dyes Chemical sunscreen filters Triclosan Heavy metals Siloxanes Synthetic musks Optical brighteners Chlorine bleach Quaternary ammonium compounds Drying alcohols Nano minerals in sprays

Standards by category

The hard blocks above apply everywhere. Each category adds its own scrutiny on top.

Skincare & Personal Care

Applied directly and often daily, so these get our strictest read — anhydrous formats are favored specifically because they need fewer preservatives.

No preservative loopholesPlastic-free packaging preferred

Every skincare standard applies, plus tighter margins on emerging-research ingredients — infant skin is thinner and absorbs more relative to body weight.

What we don't claim

We don't claim a listed product is risk-free. "Passes our standard" means it cleared our ingredient screen — it doesn't mean every possible interaction, sensitivity, or long-term outcome has been studied for every person.

We don't claim to catch everything. Our standard reflects current research and honest sourcing where brands are willing to share it. When a brand won't disclose something, we say so in "known gaps" instead of pretending it doesn't matter.

We don't claim independence from error. If we get something wrong, we correct the listing and say what changed. Being wrong quietly is worse than being wrong and fixing it.

What "nontoxic" means to us: a product formulated without ingredients we have reason to believe are harmful, made by a brand willing to be transparent about what's in it and where it came from — assessed honestly, including where it falls short.
